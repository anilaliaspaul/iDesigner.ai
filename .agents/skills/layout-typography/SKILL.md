# Layout and Typography

## Type hierarchy
- Establish a clear reading order: primary message, supporting message, key details, CTA and brand/contact footer.
- Use a deliberate type scale, line length, spacing and contrast. Do not solve hierarchy by making every line bold or decorative.
- The poster reference controls expressive display lettering and effects when the user asks to follow it. Brand fonts govern supporting copy and are the default when no reference direction exists.
- Use a small, coherent type family unless the concept justifies more. Pair display and text roles intentionally.

## English
Verify spelling, punctuation, capitalization, line breaks, kerning, tracking, contrast, legibility at target size and consistency of weights. Avoid stretched, distorted or over-decorated small copy.

## Malayalam
Use a font with verified Malayalam glyph coverage and correct shaping. Inspect conjuncts, chillu letters, vowel signs, combining marks, punctuation, baseline, word boundaries and line wrapping in the actual export. Never split a Malayalam word or detach a combining mark. Prefer shaping-capable editable text rendering over AI-generated script. Proofread with a fluent reader when available; if not, disclose that linguistic proofing remains unverified.

## Bilingual composition
Keep complete phrases in their natural language. Do not force one language into a tiny secondary caption. Give both languages intentional hierarchy and adequate space. Use script-appropriate font sizes and line heights; optical balance matters more than identical point sizes.

## BrandCraft typography method
For display titles, use a strong typeface matched to the brand and concept; for supporting text, prioritize legibility. Build effects (outline, shadow, extrusion, bevel or gradient) only when they serve the concept and match the reference. Do not apply effects indiscriminately. Use licensed or available fonts and record fallbacks in the brand kit.
